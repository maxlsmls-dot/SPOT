## The pitch in five sentences

1. The stock is priced on the May Investor Day's 2030 story (mid-teens revenue CAGR, 35–40% GM, 20%+ OI margin), and consensus has pulled that story forward into 2027: revenue +14.4% and EBIT €3.9B (+37% y/y, 17.4% margin).
2. But all three growth levers that produced the current 15% cc growth were pulled in the last 12 months and are now lapping: a 3rd US price hike (Feb-26), international hikes (Sep-25), and an emerging-market free-tier push that management has just reversed.
3. Three debates tie directly to that model: (i) ads do not "inflect to double digits" in H2-26 in any way that extrapolates into 2027, (ii) Premium revenue growth decelerates from +16% cc to ~+9% by H2-27 as ARPU growth collapses from +7% to ~+1–2% and subscriber growth slides below 8%, and (iii) the opex "surge" management calls temporary is structural, so 2027 EBIT lands ~€3.1–3.2B, 18–20% below consensus.
4. The company's own Q3 guide already shows the shape: revenue +14% cc, GM up ~130bp y/y, and yet operating margin *down* y/y (13.4% vs 13.6%) because opex is running +~25% y/y.
5. Oct 22 is where the Q4 guide has to carry "double-digit ad growth" and "opex normalising" at the same time, against a Q4-25 comp of +38M MAU and +9M subs; we think it does neither, and the 2027 reset follows in February.

## Debate 1 — Advertising: "double-digit inflection in H2-26" is a comp effect, not a business inflection

**Consensus / management:** CFO Luiga (Q2 call, 4 Aug): "we continue to expect the ads business to inflect towards double-digit growth in the second half of 2026." Investor Day made this an explicit 2030 building block. KeyBanc just cut (10/1) for a "slower ramp" but still models acceleration; Street 2027 ad growth ~+12–15%.

**Our view:** Ad growth prints mid-to-high single digits in Q3 (comp: Q3-25 was **−6%**) and stays mid-single-digit through 2027. There is no demand inflection, only a mix shift and an easy comp.

**Why:**
- Q1-26: −5% y/y (€385M). Q2-26: +3% cc (€446M), driven "by growth in impressions sold, partially offset by **softness in pricing**." Automated channels went from ~30% to ~40% of ad revenue in one quarter; that is direct-sold inventory re-priced through biddable pipes at lower CPMs, which is why 60% advertiser growth (33k active) produced 3% revenue growth.
- Ad-supported MAU grew +14% y/y (494M) while ad revenue grew +3%: **ad revenue per ad-MAU is down ~10% y/y.** Management is now deliberately throttling exactly the markets (India, Indonesia) that supplied the impressions: "introducing some friction in the ad load … and some limitations on our free tier" (Norström, Q2 call). Q3 MAU guide +11M is the smallest Q3 add since 2018. Fewer impressions + soft pricing ≠ double digits.
- The comp does the work: Q3-24 ad revenue was ~€472M; Q3-25 €447M (−6%). Even a +10% Q3-26 print (€492M) is a **two-year stack of +4%**. The Street is extrapolating a comp bounce into a 2027 growth rate.
- Industry read-through is soft: Pandora's "softer streaming music advertising" (Q2), iHeart's broadcast/network declines on advertiser caution; the only strong pocket is podcasting, where Spotify shares 50% of ad revenue with creators under the Partner Program (eligibility bar cut ~80% in Jan-26).
- The ad chief was pushed out in July 2025 ("we're behind on the plan"); the stack was rebuilt end-to-end; 15 months later growth is +3%. This is the third "second half" the ad inflection has been promised for.

**Model tie:** Ads are only ~9% of revenue, so the direct P&L hit is ~€200M of 2027 revenue (~€40M GP). The point is narrative, not dollars: ads are the proof-of-concept for the "1B MAU / monetization lever" story and the main non-pricing growth lever once price hikes lap. A single-digit Q3 print against a −6% comp removes the H2 narrative that has held the stock since Investor Day.

**What to watch 22 Oct:** Q3 ad-supported revenue (<€485M = thesis on track; ≥€495M, i.e. ≥+10%, = watch out); Q4 ad commentary; ad-MAU growth (decelerating toward +8–10%); any mention of pricing/CPM.

## Debate 2 — Premium: growth is subs × ARPU, and both are rolling over at once

**Consensus:** 2027 Premium revenue +14% built on "~8–10% subscriber growth, mid-single-digit ARPU, Music Pro/superfan upside." Management on churn: "no surprises."

**Our view:** Premium revenue growth steps down from +16% cc (Q2-26) to ~+13% (Q4-26), ~+11% (Q1-27), and ~+8–9% by H2-27. 2027 Premium +9.5%.

**Why — ARPU:**
- Q2-26 ARPU €4.89, +7.4% cc. Decomposition: **+€0.49 from price, −€0.14 from product/market mix, −€0.02 FX.** It is a pure price-hike quarter; the mix drag (family/duo/student, EM) is structural and persistent (ARPU was 0% cc in Q3-25 and +2% in Q4-25 before the hikes hit).
- The hikes lap on a schedule: Sep-25 international hikes roll off in Q4-26; the Feb-26 US hike rolls off in Q1-27. Mechanically, cc ARPU growth goes ~+7% (Q3-26) → ~+5% (Q4) → ~+3% (Q1-27) → ~+1–2% (Q2-27) absent a new broad hike.
- The next US hike is harder than the last three ($9.99→10.99→11.99→12.99 in <3 years). Spotify at $12.99 now sits *above* YouTube Music ($11.99, Apr-26 hike) and Apple Music ($11.99–12, Jul-26) and level with Amazon ($12.99). The cadence (Jul-23, Jun-24, Feb-26) points to the next move in H2-27 at the earliest, i.e. outside the 2027 P&L.
- Labels now take a bigger share of each hike. UMG's Q2: "wholesale price increases associated with Streaming 2.0 agreements contributed ~3.5pts" to subscription growth. All three majors (UMG Jan-25, WMG Feb-25, Sony Sep-25) and Merlin are on these terms. Net-of-content ARPU uplift is smaller than it was in 2023–24.
- The ARPU backfill the Street models, Music Pro / superfan tier, has no date. Bloomberg: the company "cannot agree internally on what such a plan should contain." Lossless was given away inside Premium (Sep-25). "Reserved" ticketing launched inside Premium at no charge (Jun-26). The AI covers/remix add-on has no announced price, launch date, or participating-artist list five months after Investor Day.

**Why — subscribers:**
- Y/Y subscriber growth: +12% (Q3-25) → +10% (Q4) → +9.3% (Q1-26) → +8.7% (Q2) → **+8.5% guided (Q3)**. Q2's +7M was flagged by the company as "strong global promotional campaign intake," i.e. discounted trials; Q3 guide drops back to +5M, flat y/y.
- Mature markets are saturating post-hike: Variety reported NA Premium subs declined q/q in Q1-26; Reuters/Bloomberg framed the Q2 sell-off around growth "slowing in North America and Europe." Those two regions are 61% of subs.
- The emerging-market funnel is being deliberately narrowed (free-tier friction, device deprecation), the India Premium Lite tier was scrapped six months after launch and Standard was cut 30% (May-26), and management says it is switching from "the growth lever" to "the monetization lever." You cannot throttle the top of the funnel and keep +9% subscriber growth.
- Read-across: UMG's subscription revenue slowed to +6.7% cc (ex-Downtown) with "over half" from price and 1.5pts of share loss; the stock fell 23% in a day (31 Jul). Barclays: labels' streaming growth gap to Spotify has narrowed from 17pts to 7pts. Industry subscriber growth is decelerating; Spotify is not immune, it is just a quarter behind because of its hike timing.

**Model tie:** 2027 Premium revenue €19.4B vs ~€20.3B consensus (−4%); at ~35% Premium GM that is ~€300M of gross profit.

**What to watch:** Q4 subscriber guide (<+8M = on track; ≥+9M = watch); Q4 ARPU commentary (any "consistent with Q3" language is a pass; "moderating" is the trigger); Q1-27 guide in February (we expect +3M subs, ARPU +3% cc).

## Debate 3 — The 2027 margin: "temporary" opex that is not temporary, and a GM bridge that is already slowing

**Consensus:** Opex step-up (~€200M incremental marketing + AI in 2026) is temporary and "normalises in Q4"; GM keeps compounding toward 35–40%; 2027 EBIT margin 17.4%.

**Our view:** 2027 EBIT €3.15B (14.7%). Opex grows ~+12% off the full 2026 base; GM +70bp, not +130bp.

**Why — opex:**
- Q2 opex +19% y/y ex-FX and social charges; Q3 guide implies opex ~€960–975M, ~+25% y/y (derived: €5.0B × 32.9% GM − €670M OI). Management's own Q3 guide shows **zero operating leverage**: OI margin 13.4% vs 13.6% a year ago on +14% cc growth.
- The "variable" costs are the ones that scale with the product roadmap, not against it: AI inference (DJ at ~25% of users, Prompted Playlists, the AI remix tool, agentic engineering on "Xirp") and marketing. Headcount is flat at 7,302, so there is no restructuring lever left; the growth in cost *is* the growth in the product.
- Marketing has become structural. Q2's subscriber beat was promo-driven; Q3 guide without promos drops to +5M. In saturated markets, net adds are now bought. Benchmark's cut to $650 was for precisely this: 2027 S&M growth "roughly matching growth exiting this year," putting their 2027 OI below consensus.
- Consensus 2027 implies opex +3–6% (see table). That is not a forecast, it is an extrapolation of a CFO sentence.

**Why — gross margin:**
- Q3 GM guided to 32.9%, **down 50bp q/q** from 33.4%; Q2's beat was "quarterly timing shifts related to growth investments," i.e. pushed into H2.
- The GM bridge at Investor Day: ~1/3 from music (marketplace, "quadrupled since 2021"), the rest from podcasts/audiobooks. The marketplace lever (Discovery Mode: 30% royalty haircut for algorithmic promotion) is now under a Texas AG civil investigative demand (Apr-26) and a class action in arbitration. The podcast lever runs through a Partner Program that pays out 50% of ad revenue and whose eligibility bar was cut ~80% in January. The AI add-on carries a label revenue share from day one. Every incremental GM lever has a larger leakage than the last.
- The Premium GM gain (+174bp y/y to 34.9% in Q2) came from revenue +16% cc against content costs +12%; as Premium revenue growth slides to +9–10%, that spread closes. Content cost growth is floored by the labels' Streaming 2.0 escalators.

**Model tie:** GM 33.8% vs ~34.5% (−€150M GP); opex €4.11B vs €3.86B (−€250M); revenue −€1.1B at ~34% (−€370M). EBIT €3.15B vs €3.91B.

**What to watch:** Q4 OI guide (we expect €780–800M; consensus needs ~€850–900M to hold its FY-26 €2.9B); any language retreating from "normalising in Q4"; Q4 GM guide (≤33.3% is a pass).

2. Q4 OI guide ≥€850M with GM ≥33.6%, i.e. opex really does drop q/q into the heaviest marketing quarter.

4. Q4 subscriber guide ≥+9M without a promotional-campaign caveat.
5. A material step-up in buybacks (≥$1B per quarter) or a capital-return framework at the Q4 print.
