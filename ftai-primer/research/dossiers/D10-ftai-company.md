## 2. Mechanics

### 2.1 External management and the internalization

**External management** is a structure in which a listed company has no employees of its own; a separate **manager** (here FIG LLC, a subsidiary of Fortress Investment Group) supplies the executives, staff and investment process under a **management agreement**, and is paid a **management fee** (a periodic fee, typically a percentage of the company's equity or assets) plus a performance payment. At FTAI the performance payment was an **incentive allocation** paid to a Fortress affiliate called the **Master GP** (the filings refer to "Fortress Worldwide Transportation and Infrastructure Master GP LLC"; the exact entity name is from the orchestrator's chain map, not re-verified here) under a **Services and Profit Sharing Agreement**: a share of FTAI's distributable returns above a hurdle, as is standard for Fortress-managed vehicles. The 10-Q for Q3 2024 confirms both the Management Agreement and the Services and Profit Sharing Agreement were the two contracts under which "accrued but not yet paid compensation and reimbursable expenses" were owed at termination [S8][S10].

**Internalization** means the company terminates the management agreement, hires the manager's people directly and pays the manager for giving up its future fee stream. Mechanically, on 28 May 2024 FTAI entered into definitive agreements with the Former Manager and the Master GP; the Management Agreement terminated effective 28 May 2024, after which "the Company was no longer required to pay management fees or incentive distributions" [S8]. Consideration, per the 10-Q: "the Company agreed to pay $300.0 million to its Former Manager (for itself and on behalf of the Master GP, as applicable), issued 1,866,949 ordinary shares valued at $150.0 million at closing, with the remaining balance paid in cash on June 17, 2024" [S8][S9]. The 8-K adds that FTAI also paid all accrued but unpaid compensation and reimbursable expenses under both agreements plus "certain other expenses" [S10].

*Worked example — the implied share price of the equity consideration.* $150,000,000 ÷ 1,866,949 shares = $80.35 per share. That is the value the parties assigned to each share at closing; whether it equalled the market price on 28 May 2024 is not confirmed here (Unknown U-6). The share component also tells you the dilution: 1,866,949 shares against roughly 100M outstanding at the time (the exact pre-deal count is not retrieved) is about 1.9%.

Why it matters for reading the financials: before 28 May 2024, management fees and incentive allocations appeared as expense lines (and the incentive allocation as a distribution-like charge); after that date they are replaced by ordinary salaries, bonuses and equity awards reported in the proxy. Comparisons of 2023 vs 2025 operating expenses are therefore not like-for-like. The fee terms and the amounts paid by year were sought but not retrieved (Unknown U-5).

### 2.2 The 2022 spin-off of FTAI Infrastructure

A **spin-off** is a pro-rata distribution of the shares of a subsidiary to the parent's shareholders, after which the subsidiary is a separate listed company. FTAI Infrastructure Inc. was formed as a limited liability company on 13 Dec 2021 to hold the infrastructure businesses. On 1 Aug 2022, FTAI distributed one share of FTAI Infrastructure common stock for each FTAI common share held at the close of business on 21 Jul 2022 (the **record date**), and FTAI Infrastructure began trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market as "FIP" [S16][S18]. From that date FTAI's financials present the infrastructure businesses (Jefferson Terminal, Repauno, Transtar, Long Ridge — names per the orchestrator's brief; their individual figures were not retrieved, Unknown U-11) as discontinued operations, so FY2021 and prior consolidated totals are not comparable to FY2023 onward without restatement.

### 2.3 The 2022 redomiciliation to the Cayman Islands

A **Cayman Islands exempted company** is a company incorporated in the Cayman Islands that conducts its business mainly outside the islands; it is a common holding-company form for aircraft lessors. FTAI Aviation Ltd. was formed on 8 Dec 2017 and became the surviving parent on completion of an Agreement and Plan of Merger dated 10 Nov 2022 between Fortress Transportation and Infrastructure Investors LLC (a Delaware LLC) and FTAI Aviation Ltd.; the LLC's public common shares "were exchanged automatically for shares of FTAI Aviation Ltd." [S14][S17]. The merger was put to shareholders in a definitive merger proxy (DEFM14A, 2022) [S17]; an S-4 registration by "FTAI Finance Holdco Ltd." (CIK 1942420) was filed the same year, consistent with exchanging the old issuer's notes for new-issuer notes [S17]. The company's stated reasons for the redomiciliation are in the DEFM14A and were not retrieved (Unknown U-12).

### 2.4 Segments and what each revenue line is

The FY2025 10-K reports two segments (definitions per the 10-K as summarized in the chain map) [S1][chain map]:

- **Aerospace Products** — "develops and manufactures, repairs and refurbishes, and sells aircraft engines and aftermarket components, primarily for the CFM56-7B, CFM56-5B and V2500" engines. It sells refurbished **modules** (the separable sub-assemblies of a jet engine — fan, high-pressure compressor, high-pressure turbine, low-pressure turbine — each with its own serial number and life record), whole engines, and **PMA parts** (Parts Manufacturer Approval: FAA-approved replacement parts made by someone other than the engine's original manufacturer) via its joint venture with Chromalloy.
- **Aviation Leasing** — "owns and manages aircraft and engines and leases and sells them, directly and through an equity-method investment." At 31 Dec 2025 it held 290 assets: 47 aircraft and 243 engines, including 8 aircraft and 17 engines in Russia [chain map].

### 2.5 Adjusted EBITDA, the company's headline non-GAAP measure

**EBITDA** is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. **Adjusted EBITDA** is a company-defined variant that also removes items management considers non-recurring or non-cash and, at FTAI, adds back the company's pro-rata share of Adjusted EBITDA from unconsolidated (equity-method) investments. FTAI reports it at consolidated level and by segment, and sets guidance in it. The exact FY2025 definition and the reconciliation line items (e.g., equity-based compensation, acquisition and transaction expenses, internalization costs, asset impairment, changes in fair value of non-hedge derivatives, dividends on preferred shares, the equity-method add-back) were in the FY2025 earnings release and 10-K but were not returned by the searches (Unknown U-1). The reader should expect the reconciliation to start from net income attributable to shareholders and to add back preferred dividends; that convention matters because it means Adjusted EBITDA is before the ~$6M a year of Series D dividends and the Series B/C dividends while those were outstanding.

### 2.6 Why operating cash flow is negative while the company is profitable

Under US GAAP, cash spent buying assets for **sale** (inventory) is an operating outflow; cash spent buying assets for **lease** (leasing equipment, depreciated over time) is an investing outflow. A company that is rapidly building an inventory of engines, modules and parts to refurbish and sell will show operating cash outflows even while reporting net income, because the inventory build runs through working capital. FTAI's net cash from operating activities was +$128,982K in 2023, −$187,956K in 2024 and −$310,745K in 2025 [S1]. The 10-K's working-capital detail (change in inventory, receivables, deferred revenue) and the investing lines (acquisition of leasing equipment, acquisition of property, plant and equipment, proceeds from asset sales) were not retrieved (Unknown U-2), so the split between inventory build and other drivers cannot be shown here.

### 2.7 The debt instruments

**Senior unsecured notes** are bonds that rank ahead of equity and subordinated debt but are not backed by specific collateral; they pay a fixed coupon (normally semi-annually), repay the full principal at maturity (a **bullet**), and are typically **callable** by the issuer after a non-call period at a declining premium. FTAI's notes are the bulk of its debt. The FY2025 10-K and Q1 2026 10-Q report ($ thousands, as presented) [S1][S2]:

| Series | Coupon | Maturity | Balance reported | As of |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Senior Notes due 2028 | 5.50% | 1 May 2028 | 1,000,995 | 31 Dec 2025 (10-K) |
| Senior Notes due 2030 | 7.875% ("7.88%") | 1 Dec 2030 | 497,470 | 31 Dec 2025 (10-K) |
| Senior Notes due 2031 | 7.00% | 1 Dec 2031 | 717,752 | 31 Mar 2026 (10-Q) |
| Senior Notes due 2032 | 6.875% ("6.88%") | 1 Dec 2032 | 823,184 | 31 Mar 2026 (10-Q) |
| Senior Notes due 2033 | 5.875% ("5.88%") | 15 Apr 2033 | 497,784 | 31 Dec 2025 (10-K) |
| Revolving Credit Facility | Base Rate + 1.75% or Adjusted Term SOFR + 2.75% | 22 May 2027 | 0 drawn | 31 Dec 2025 (10-K) |

The 2028, 2030 and 2033 balances sit just above or below round numbers ($1,000M, $500M, $500M), which is the signature of principal carried at a small premium or discount; the 2031 and 2032 balances are not round, which usually indicates partial exchanges or add-ons (Unknown U-3: issue dates, original principal, and whether the figures are principal or carrying amount). The search did not confirm whether the five series were all outstanding at both dates; the table mixes the two dates as the search returned them (Tension T-1).

A **revolving credit facility** ("revolver") is a bank line the company can draw and repay repeatedly up to a commitment, paying interest on drawn amounts (here a floating rate: the bank's base rate plus 1.75% or **SOFR**, the Secured Overnight Financing Rate, plus 2.75%) and a commitment fee on the undrawn portion. FTAI's revolver was undrawn at 31 Dec 2025 and matures 22 May 2027 [S1]; its commitment size was not retrieved (Unknown U-3).

*Worked example — annual cash interest on the notes, assuming the reported balances are principal.* 1,000,995 × 5.50% = 55,055; 497,470 × 7.875% = 39,176; 717,752 × 7.00% = 50,243; 823,184 × 6.875% = 56,594; 497,784 × 5.875% = 29,245. Sum ≈ $230.3M a year of coupon, or ≈6.5% blended on ≈$3,537M. This is an illustration from the table, not a reported figure; reported interest expense was not retrieved (Unknown U-1).

### 2.8 The preferred shares

**Preferred shares** rank ahead of ordinary shares for dividends and in liquidation but carry no vote in normal circumstances. FTAI's are all **cumulative** (a missed dividend accrues and must be paid before any ordinary dividend), **perpetual** (no maturity) and **redeemable** at the company's option at the **liquidation preference** of $25.00 per share plus accrued dividends, from a first call date. Two coupon designs appear [S1]:

- **Fixed-to-floating** (Series A 8.25%, Series B 8.00%): a fixed coupon until the first call date, after which the dividend floats at a reference rate plus a fixed spread, reset quarterly.
- **Fixed-rate reset** (Series C 8.25%, Series D 9.50%): a fixed coupon until the first reset date, then a new fixed coupon for the next five years equal to the then five-year US Treasury yield plus a fixed spread.

*Worked example — quarterly dividend per preferred share.* Series D: 9.50% × $25.00 ÷ 4 = $0.59375, which the 10-Q rounds to $0.59 per share for Q1 2025; Series C: 8.25% × $25.00 ÷ 4 = $0.515625 → $0.52 per share, matching the filing [S19]. For the 2,600,000 Series D shares the annual cash cost is 2,600,000 × $25 × 9.5% = $6.175M, i.e. ≈$1.54M a quarter.

Redemptions to date: in Oct 2024 FTAI redeemed all 4,180,000 Series A shares at $25.00 plus $1.6M of accumulated and unpaid distributions, a total outlay of ≈$106.1M [S1]. On 15 May 2026 it announced the full redemption of the Series C shares [S20] (amount and date of redemption not read; Unknown U-4). The FY2025 10-K summary returned by the search states Series B and Series C remained outstanding at year-end 2025; Series B's share count and floating spread were not retrieved (Unknown U-4). Tickers: the EDGAR company lookup returned by the search uses the ticker "FTAIO" as a lookup key [S6 result list], which confirms at least one preferred ticker in that family exists; the mapping of FTAIO/FTAIP/FTAIN/FTAIM to series is a recollection, unverified.

### 2.9 Net debt and leverage — an illustration, not the company's definition

**Net debt** is total debt less cash. At 31 Mar 2026: $3,451,087K long-term debt, net − $412,240K cash = $3,038,847K ≈ $3.04B [S2]. A **leverage ratio** divides debt (or net debt) by a measure of annual earnings; the company's and the rating agencies' definitions (gross vs net, whether preferred counts as debt, which EBITDA) were not retrieved (Unknown U-7). For scale only: the Q4 2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $277.2M [S3] annualizes to $1,108.8M, so net debt ÷ annualized Adjusted EBITDA ≈ 2.7x; using Q2 2026's $291.4M annualized ($1,165.6M) gives ≈2.6x. These are arithmetic from two reported numbers, not reported ratios.

### 2.10 Share count, dividends and the repurchase program

**Basic EPS** divides net income attributable to ordinary shareholders by the weighted-average ordinary shares outstanding; **diluted EPS** adds shares that would be issued if restricted stock units (RSUs) and performance stock units (PSUs) vested. Implied weighted shares: Q4 2025 $111.9M ÷ $1.09 ≈ 102.7M basic and ÷ $1.08 ≈ 103.6M diluted; Q2 2026 $117.6M ÷ $1.15 ≈ 102.3M basic and ÷ $1.13 ≈ 104.1M diluted [S3][S4]. The 2026 proxy's ownership table implies ≈102.6M shares outstanding at the record date (13,865,244 ÷ 13.51%) [S6]. So the share count has been roughly flat at ≈102–103M since the 1,866,949-share internalization issuance in mid-2024, with dilution limited to equity awards.

Ordinary dividends: $0.30 per share per quarter through 2025 (Q1 2025 confirmed [S19]; the Feb 2025 release title confirms $0.30 for Q4 2024 [S23]); raised to $0.40 with the Q4 2025 results on 25 Feb 2026 [S3][S22]; raised to $0.50 with the Q2 2026 results on 29 Jul 2026 [S4]. The Q1 2026 declared rate was not retrieved (Unknown U-8).

A **share repurchase program** is a board authorization to buy back up to a stated dollar amount of shares over time at management's discretion; it is not a commitment. FTAI announced a $500M program on 15 Sep 2026 [S21] (terms, duration and funding not read; Unknown U-8).

### 2.11 The audit committee review mechanism

When a short seller publishes allegations about accounting, the board's **audit committee** (independent directors who oversee financial reporting and the external auditor) can commission a **review** by independent counsel and **forensic accountants** (accountants who test transactions and records for misstatement). Muddy Waters Research published its report on 15 Jan 2025; on 18 Jan 2025 the Audit Committee "determined to commence a review (which included the engagement of independent advisors) in response to the assertions" [S12]. The company later announced the committee had "completed its review, conducted by independent legal and forensic accounting advisors ... and determined that the allegations made against the Company are without merit," and that it expected to file its Form 10-K timely [S13]; the 10-Q language is that "the internal investigation concluded that the allegations of misconduct were all without merit" [S7]. The allegations themselves (per the orchestrator's chain map: whole-engine sales booked as module sales, and expense reclassification) were not read from the Muddy Waters document in this session; both the allegation and the finding are recorded here as the two sides (Tension T-6).

## 5. Constraints and bottlenecks (company level)

- **Debt maturities.** The revolver matures 22 May 2027 and the 5.50% 2028 notes (≈$1.0B) on 1 May 2028 [S1]; both fall before the 2027 guidance year ends. Refinancing terms will depend on the credit ratings, which were not retrieved (Unknown U-7).
- **Cost of debt.** Coupons range from 5.50% to 7.875%; the illustrative coupon load on the five series is ≈$230M a year (Section 2.7), against Adjusted EBITDA of ≈$277–291M a quarter [S3][S4].
- **Cash generation.** Operating cash flow has been negative for two years because growth in Aerospace Products is funded through inventory [S1]; the asset-light shift in Aviation Leasing is meant to release leasing capital into SCI vehicles, lowering Aviation Leasing's own Adjusted EBITDA guidance by $100M for 2026 [S4].
- **Stand-ready obligations.** MRE contracts oblige FTAI to supply replacement CFM56-5B and -7B engines as customers' engines fail [S7]; the company must hold a pool of serviceable engines against that obligation.
- **Customer concentration.** 16% and 11% of revenue from two Aerospace Products customers in Q3 2025 [S7].
- **Russia.** 8 aircraft and 17 engines were in Russia at 31 Dec 2025 [chain map]; the 10-K lists new or stricter export controls and sanctions against Russia, and responses to them, as able to "delay or prevent access to certain assets" [S1].
- **Leverage as a stated risk.** The 10-K lists the company's "ability to successfully utilize leverage in connection with its investments" among risk factors [S1].
- **Governance structure.** Classified board; combined CEO and Chairman [S6].

## 6. Tensions

- **T-1 — Note balances vs. reported total debt.** The five senior-note balances as returned sum to $3,537,185K (three at 31 Dec 2025, two at 31 Mar 2026), while the 10-Q reports long-term debt, net of $3,451,087K at 31 Mar 2026 [S1][S2]. The $86M gap could be unamortized issuance costs and discounts, a change in balances between the two dates, or a series partly retired; the search did not say which. Resolve with the debt note in the Q1 2026 10-Q.
- **T-2 — Internalization consideration framed two ways.** The Q3 2024 10-Q summary says the company "agreed to pay $150.0 million to the Former Manager" [S8]; the same filing elsewhere, and the Q2 2024 10-Q, describe total consideration of $300.0M comprising 1,866,949 shares valued at $150.0M plus cash [S8][S9]; the 8-K adds accrued compensation, reimbursable expenses and "certain other expenses" on top [S10]. The $150M is the cash leg; total cash paid exceeded it by the accrued and reimbursable amounts, which were not quantified in the results.
- **T-3 — Revenue lines do not sum to total.** The five named lines total $2,496,898K against total revenues of $2,507,409K for 2025 [S1]; the $10,511K residual is presumed to be an "other revenue" line that the search did not return.
- **T-4 — Segment revenue vs. income-statement lines.** Q2 2026 Aerospace Products segment revenue is $875.0M [S4]; the FY2025 10-K's "Aerospace products revenue" line is a product line that excludes MRE contract revenue [S1]. Whether MRE sits in the Aerospace Products segment (the chain map says MRE is "its Maintenance, Repair and Exchange business" serving SCI aircraft) determines whether Q1 2026's $522.6M or $743.8M is the comparable base.
- **T-5 — Who is COO.** The 2026 proxy names Stacy Kuperus as Chief Operating Officer [S6]; David Moreno, who appears in the same ownership table with 54,232 shares, held the COO title in earlier years (recollection, unverified). His current title was not returned.
- **T-6 — Muddy Waters allegations vs. audit committee finding.** The short report (15 Jan 2025) alleged, per the chain map, that whole-engine sales were booked as module sales and that expenses were reclassified; the Audit Committee, through independent legal and forensic accounting advisors, "determined that the allegations made against the Company are without merit" [S12][S13][S7]. Neither the report nor the committee's detailed findings were read in this session.
- **T-7 — Series B status.** The FY2025 10-K summary returned by the search says Series B remains outstanding, while the company's redemption sequence (Series A in Oct 2024, Series C in May 2026) skipped B; nothing retrieved explains why the lower-coupon fixed-to-floating series was kept, or what its current floating rate is.

## 8. Terms introduced

- **External management / manager / management agreement:** structure where a listed company's staff and strategy are supplied by a separate firm under contract for a fee.
- **Management fee:** periodic payment to the manager, usually a percentage of equity or assets.
- **Incentive allocation:** performance payment to the manager's affiliate (the Master GP) as a share of returns above a hurdle.
- **Master GP:** the Fortress affiliate entitled to the incentive allocation under the Services and Profit Sharing Agreement.
- **Internalization:** terminating the management agreement and paying the manager for its foregone fees while hiring its people directly.
- **Spin-off / record date:** pro-rata distribution of a subsidiary's shares to holders of record on a set date.
- **Cayman Islands exempted company:** Cayman-incorporated company doing business mainly outside the islands.
- **Module:** separable, serialized sub-assembly of a jet engine (fan, compressor, turbine).
- **PMA (Parts Manufacturer Approval):** FAA approval for a non-OEM replacement part.
- **MRE (Maintenance, Repair and Exchange):** contract under which FTAI swaps a customer's failed engine for a serviceable one.
- **Stand-ready obligation:** a contractual duty to supply (here, replacement engines) on demand.
- **Maintenance reserves:** per-hour/cycle payments by lessees toward future shop visits, recognized as revenue when no longer refundable.
- **Equity-method investment:** a stake (typically 20–50%) accounted for by recording FTAI's share of the investee's earnings rather than consolidating it.
- **EBITDA / Adjusted EBITDA:** earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization; the company-defined adjusted version.
- **Operating vs investing cash flow (inventory vs leasing equipment):** purchases of assets for sale are operating outflows; purchases of assets for lease are investing outflows.
- **Senior unsecured notes / bullet / callable:** uncollateralized bonds repaying principal at maturity, redeemable early at a premium.
- **Revolving credit facility / SOFR:** a reusable bank line priced at a floating reference rate plus spread.
- **Preferred shares; cumulative; perpetual; redeemable; liquidation preference:** equity ranking ahead of ordinary shares, with accruing dividends, no maturity, issuer call right, and a fixed per-share claim ($25).
- **Fixed-to-floating / fixed-rate reset:** preferred coupon designs that switch to a floating rate, or re-fix every five years off Treasuries, after a first date.
- **Net debt; leverage ratio:** debt less cash; debt divided by an earnings measure.
- **Basic / diluted EPS; RSU; PSU:** per-share earnings before and after potential share issuance; time-vested and performance-vested share units.
- **Share repurchase program:** board authorization to buy back shares up to a dollar limit.
- **Classified board:** staggered three-year director terms.
- **Audit committee review; forensic accountants:** independent-director-led investigation using specialist accountants.
- **Asset-light model:** owning fewer leased assets and instead earning fees and maintenance revenue from vehicles that own them.
