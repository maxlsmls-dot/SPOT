## 1. Summary

- SCI is FTAI's asset-management arm: it raises third-party capital into closed-end vehicles that buy
  on-lease, mid-life Boeing 737NG and Airbus A320ceo aircraft; FTAI manages the vehicles as "Servicer",
  co-invests as a limited partner, and supplies every engine shop visit for the fleet through its

## 2. Mechanics

### 2.1 What an aviation "strategic capital" vehicle is

A **special purpose vehicle (SPV)** is a legal entity created to hold a defined pool of assets and the
debt secured on them, insulated from the sponsor's other liabilities. In SCI, each vehicle is a
**partnership**: third-party investors are **limited partners (LPs)** who commit capital, and FTAI is
both an LP (19% of the 2025 Partnership [S13]) and the **Servicer**, the party contracted to manage the
assets. FTAI's filings call the first vehicle the "2025 Partnership" and say it acquires aircraft through
"special purpose entities (SPVs) of the 2025 Partnership" [S12], so the structure is a partnership on
top of asset-owning SPVs, which is how aircraft lessors are usually organized (each aircraft or
sub-pool in its own entity so that lenders and lessees deal with a clean owner).

A **closed-end vehicle** raises a fixed amount of **equity commitments** (promises to fund when called),
has a **hard cap** (the maximum the sponsor will accept), invests during a **deployment** or investment
period, then stops buying and enters a **harvest phase** in which it collects rent, distributes cash to
LPs, and sells assets until it winds up. "Fully committed" means every dollar of equity has been
allocated to signed acquisitions, even if some closings are still pending. The 2025 SPV was described as
fully committed and in harvest phase as of July–August 2026, with its first quarterly distribution on
30 June 2026 [S8, S9]. The term and exit mechanics of the 2025 Partnership were not located (Unknown U15).

### 2.2 The two layers of debt

**Asset-level debt** is borrowed by the SPVs and secured on the aircraft and their leases, with no
recourse to FTAI. The Feb 2025 commitment was $2.5B of asset-level debt, with ATLAS SP Partners
(Apollo's structured-products business) committing $2.0B directly and acting as structuring agent and
lead arranger, and Deutsche Bank AG, New York Branch co-leading [S2].

A **warehouse facility** is a revolving, asset-level credit line that funds acquisitions one by one as a
vehicle buys them, before the portfolio is large and seasoned enough to be refinanced in a term deal
(typically an **asset-backed securitization, ABS**: bonds tranched by seniority and repaid from the pool's
rents). An **accordion** is a pre-agreed option to enlarge the facility if lenders agree to fund more. The
2026 SPV's facility is $2.0B drawn capacity syndicated among 13 banks with a $1.0B accordion, so up to
$3.0B [S9]. **Syndicated** means the risk is spread across many lenders under one agreement; the
**co-structuring agents** (ATLAS SP and Deutsche Bank here) design the deal and lead the group.

FTAI stated that its "Strategic Capital vehicles have now raised $5.5 billion of warehouse financing in
less than two years" [S9]. The two disclosed commitments sum to $4.5B ($2.5B + $2.0B). The $5.5B figure
matches $2.5B + $3.0B (counting the accordion) but the release does not say how the figure is built
(Tension T2, Unknown U11).

of 4.0 / 6.0 = 67%, i.e. $2 of debt for each $1 of equity. If only the $2.5B Feb 2025 commitment were

not disclosed in the documents located (Unknown U11). Per-aircraft capital at $6.0B / 300 aircraft =
$20M; the seed portfolio was $549M / 46 = $11.9M per aircraft [S1]; Q1 2025 sales were $58.9M / 4 =
$14.7M [S12]; the first 101 acquisitions were $1.4B / 101 = $13.9M [S4]; the $2.1B then under contract
for 89 aircraft (190 − 101) was $23.6M [S4]. The range is wide (Tension T5); "total capital" may include
reserves for engine exchanges, and later purchases may skew to younger or A320ceo aircraft. Not stated.

### 2.4 What the vehicles buy and why the engines matter

A **737NG** (Boeing 737 Next Generation: -600/-700/-800/-900, built roughly 1997–2019) is powered by two
CFM56-7B engines. An **A320ceo** (Airbus A320 "current engine option" family, built roughly 1988–2020)
is powered by two CFM56-5B or two IAE V2500 engines. **Mid-life** means roughly the middle of a 25-30
year economic life, when an aircraft's value is increasingly in its engines and the engines are approaching
or passing a **shop visit** (a scheduled teardown and restoration of an engine, costed in the single-digit
millions of dollars; see D1 and D2). **On-lease** means the aircraft is already under an operating lease
to an airline, so the buyer acquires a rent stream and a return-condition obligation, not an empty aircraft.

Each release repeats the same sentence: engines owned by the vehicle "will be powered exclusively via
engine and module exchanges with FTAI's Maintenance, Repair and Exchange (MRE) business" [S2, S3, S9].
A **module** is one of the separable sections of a CFM56 (fan, high-pressure compressor, high-pressure
turbine, low-pressure turbine, plus accessory gearbox); a **module exchange** swaps a time-expired module
for a freshly refurbished one from FTAI's pool, so the aircraft returns to service in days instead of
waiting months for its own engine to be overhauled; an **engine exchange** does the same at whole-engine
level. The exchange is a sale by FTAI of the replacement module or engine (booked in Aerospace Products),
usually with the run-out unit taken back as feedstock. The 10-Q describes the arrangement as an "MRE
Contract" under which FTAI "sells CFM56-5B, CFM56-7B and V2500 commercial aircraft engines and related
modules to the special purpose entities (SPVs) of the 2025 Partnership" [S12, S13]. FTAI said in Feb 2025
that MRE addresses a $22B total addressable market in which it held ~5% share as of 2024 [S2].

### 2.5 Money flows between FTAI and a vehicle (with where each lands)

1. **Aircraft sales from FTAI's balance sheet into the vehicle.** The 2025 Partnership "acquires on-lease
   narrowbody aircraft from the Company ... with aircraft being sold to the 2025 Partnership for a gain"
   [S11]. FTAI accounts for these under **ASC 610-20** (Other Income — Gains and Losses from the
   Derecognition of Nonfinancial Assets), stating they are "non-recurring in nature and not considered part
   of the Company's ordinary activities" [S11]. Under that standard, the gain (price minus carrying value)
   appears as a gain line outside revenue rather than as "asset sales revenue". FTAI's Adjusted EBITDA for
   Aviation Leasing has historically included gains on asset sales (see D6 and D10 for the definition).
   Figures: Q1 2025 4 aircraft, gain $10.9M, proceeds $58.9M; Q2 2025 33 aircraft, gain $34.6M (1H 2025:
   37 aircraft, $45.5M); Q3 2025 8 aircraft, gain $4.6M; FY2025 gain $46.4M; Q1 2026 proceeds $117.3M
   [S11–S16]. The 46-aircraft seed package was priced at "an estimated net purchase price of $549 million"
   [S1]. How prices are set (appraisal, third-party, LP consent) was not located (Unknown U5).

2. **Servicing fees.** FTAI "as the Servicer, provides aircraft management services to the 2025
   Partnership, and receives customary, market-based compensation for providing such services" [S11].
   Disclosed: $10.2M for FY2025; $6.988M for Q2 2026; $12.849M for 1H 2026 (so $5.861M for Q1 2026,
   derived) [S11, S13]. No rate or base is disclosed (Unknown U1). The income-statement line (most likely
   "Other revenue" in Aviation Leasing) was not confirmed (Unknown U8).

3. **Engine and module sales under the MRE Contract.** Revenue is booked in Aerospace Products under
   **ASC 606** (revenue from contracts with customers). Because FTAI owns 19% of the buyer, the profit on
   those sales is partly or wholly **eliminated**: "the elimination of profit on sales of engines and modules
   to the 2025 Partnership under ASC 606. The profit from the MRE Contract revenue is eliminated through
   equity method earnings and will be recognized over time as the 2025 Partnership generates income from
   leasing and sales activities" [S13]. Eliminations: $10.0M (Q1 2026), $6.597M (Q2 2026), $16.597M (1H
   2026) [S12, S13]. The gross MRE revenue to the Partnership was not located (Unknown U6).

   Worked inference on what the elimination implies (two cases, because the filing does not say which):
   - If the elimination is proportional to FTAI's 19% stake (the usual treatment of intra-entity profit
     with an equity-method investee), 1H 2026 gross profit on MRE sales to the Partnership ≈ $16.6M / 0.19
     ≈ $87M.
   - If 100% of the profit is deferred, gross profit ≈ $16.6M.
   The sentence "recognized over time as the 2025 Partnership generates income" is consistent with either.
   Recorded as Tension T7.

4. **Equity-method income.** FTAI's 19% LP interest is accounted for under the **equity method**: the
   investment is carried at cost plus FTAI's share of the investee's earnings minus distributions and
   eliminations, with the share shown on one line ("equity in earnings of unconsolidated entities").
   FTAI says its share "is based on the contractual profit-sharing arrangement" rather than a flat 19%
   [S13], which is the language one would use if the partnership agreement has a **waterfall** (an
   ordered allocation of distributions, typically return of capital, then a preferred return or **hurdle**,
   then a **promote** or **carried interest**, a disproportionate share of profit to the sponsor). Whether
   a promote exists, and its terms, are not disclosed (Unknown U2). Carrying values: $281.74M at 31 Dec
   2025; $279.42M at 31 Mar 2026; $365.49M at 30 Jun 2026 [S11–S13]. The Q2 2026 step-up of $86M
   (derived) coincides with completion of deployment; 19% × $2.0B = $380M of total commitment (derived).

5. **Gains to the vehicle on aircraft sales in harvest.** Not a direct FTAI stream, but FTAI's 19% share
   and any promote flow through equity-method income; a sales fee is typical in the industry (Carlyle: 3%
   of sales proceeds [S19]; Willis: 3.0% of net proceeds [S21]) but is not disclosed for SCI.

### 2.6 Consolidation and segments

The FY2025 10-K has two reportable segments, Aviation Leasing and Aerospace Products; Aviation Leasing
"owns and manages aircraft and engines and leases and sells them, directly and through an equity-method
investment" (chain-map reading of the 10-K [S11]). SCI is not a reportable segment. The Q2 2026 10-Q
describes SCI as "an asset management business that manages third-party capital to invest in on-lease
aircraft" and says the company expects its primary investment activities going forward to be through
SCI [S13]. The 2025 Partnership is unconsolidated; FTAI records a 19% equity-method investment. A
**variable interest entity (VIE)** analysis (whether the sponsor is the primary beneficiary of an entity
whose equity holders lack control or sufficient equity) would have been required; the reasoning was not
located (Unknown U18). Related-party disclosures in the 10-Qs cover: servicing fees received, aircraft
sales and proceeds, engine/module sales and profit eliminations, and the carrying value [S12, S13].

## 5. Worked examples

### 5.1 Annual fee income on a $6B vehicle

**(a) Disclosed run-rate.** Q2 2026 servicing fees $6.988M × 4 = $27.95M per year; 1H 2026 $12.849M × 2
= $25.70M per year [S13]. Relative to ~$6.0B committed total capital: 27.95 / 6,000 = 0.47% (or 0.43%
on the 1H basis). Relative to $2.0B equity: 1.40% (1.28%). Caveat: Q2 2026 is the first quarter after
full deployment, so the run-rate may still be rising; and the base (equity, invested cost, rents) is
not disclosed.

**(b) Carlyle-style ABS servicing scale.** Requires an annual rent figure. Assumption: a mid-life
narrowbody earns monthly rent of 0.9% of its purchase price (a **lease rate factor**, monthly rent ÷
asset value; D6 should supply a sourced range). Annual rent = 0.9% × 12 × $6.0B = $648M.
- Servicing at 2% of rents = $13.0M.
- Incentive at the 5% cap = $32.4M.
- Sales fee at 3% on an assumed $600M of disposals per year in harvest = $18.0M.
- Total if all three apply at the top of the range = $63.4M; servicing alone = $13.0M.

**(c) Private-fund scale.** Assumption: 1.5% management fee on $2.0B committed equity = $30.0M per year,
plus **carried interest** of 20% of profit above an 8% **hurdle** (the preferred return LPs receive
before the sponsor shares in profit). Illustrative: if the vehicle returns $2.0B of capital plus $800M
of profit over its life and the hurdle consumes $500M of that, carry = 20% × $300M = $60M, realized at
exit, not annually. Whether SCI has any such terms is Unknown U2.

**(d) Willis WEST scale** (for contrast, because it is an internal fee inside a consolidated ABS):
13.5% × $648M = $87.5M per year. No evidence places SCI near this.

Reading: the disclosed $26–28M run-rate falls between the servicing-only ABS figure (~$13M) and the
private-fund management-fee figure (~$30M), but the comparison depends entirely on the rent assumption.

### 5.2 Engine maintenance stream from 300 mid-life narrowbodies

All 737NG and A320ceo aircraft have two engines: 300 aircraft × 2 = 600 engines. The rest of the chain
needs three inputs that were not disclosed for the SCI fleets and must come from D1, D2 and D5:

| Input | Assumption used here | What would replace it |
|---|---|---|
| Shop visits per engine per year | 1 / 6 (one restoration every six years) | D1/D2: mid-life CFM56-7B/-5B and V2500 interval data |
| Modules exchanged per shop-visit event | 1.5 (FTAI's model replaces the time-expired modules, not the whole engine) | D5: disclosed module-per-event ratio, if any |
| FTAI revenue per module | $1.0–2.0M range | D5: module pricing; ceiling check below |

Arithmetic:
- Engine events per year = 600 / 6 = 100.
- Modules per year = 100 × 1.5 = 150. For scale, FTAI's 2026 forecast is 1,200 modules and its stated
  capacity 3,000 modules per year (chain map, from the Q2 2026 release [S8]); 150 would be ~12.5% of the
  2026 forecast.
- Revenue per year = 150 × $1.0–2.0M = $150–300M.
- Ceiling check (derived): Q2 2026 Aerospace Products revenue $875.0M ÷ 296 modules refurbished =
  $2.96M per module, but that revenue includes whole-engine sales and other product, so true per-module
  revenue is below $2.96M; the $1.0–2.0M band is an assumption inside that ceiling.
- Segment-margin check: Q2 2026 Aerospace Products Adj. EBITDA ÷ revenue = $249.7M / $875.0M = 28.5%
  [S8]. Applied to $150–300M gives $43–86M of segment EBITDA per year before the equity-method
  elimination of profit on sales to a 19%-owned buyer.
- Cross-check against disclosed eliminations: 1H 2026 eliminations of $16.6M [S13]. Under the
  proportional case (÷ 0.19) that implies ~$87M of half-year gross profit on MRE sales to the Partnership,
  i.e. ~$174M annualized, above the top of the band derived here; under the full-elimination case it
  implies ~$33M annualized, below the band. Which is right depends on the elimination method (Tension
  T7) and on whether 1H 2026 included catch-up exchanges on newly acquired aircraft. The gap is the
  reason the inputs above need sourcing.

### 5.3 Reconciling the seed sale

aircraft with gains of $10.9M + $34.6M + $4.6M = $50.1M [S14–S16]. FY2025 gain stated $46.4M [S11].

## 6. Constraints and bottlenecks

- **Equity fund-raising cadence.** Each vehicle needs a new equity raise; the 2025 SPV took from Dec 2024
  to Oct 2025 to close at $2.0B [S1, S4]; SCI II fundraising was launched by Feb 2026 "with anchor investor
  commitments" [S6], and its warehouse closed Aug 2026 [S9] with the equity size undisclosed (Unknown U4).
- **Debt market access.** Both vehicles rely on bank warehouses led by ATLAS SP/Apollo and Deutsche Bank
  [S2, S9]; take-out into ABS (see the May 2026 "Inaugural Asset-Backed Securitization" release, S26,
  whose issuer was not confirmed, Unknown U12) is the usual path to term funding.
- **Supply of on-lease mid-life aircraft.** The 2025 SPV bought 300+ aircraft in ~18 months [S9]; the
  launch release framed the market as supporting "$3.0+ billion of capital annually" [S1]. The pipeline
  for the 2026 SPV begins with a 17-aircraft sale-leaseback [S10].
- **MRE capacity.** The exclusive exchange arrangement makes the vehicles dependent on FTAI's module
  output (296 modules in Q2 2026; 1,200 forecast for 2026; 3,000 stated capacity, chain map/S8) and
  makes FTAI's Aerospace Products revenue partly dependent on the vehicles' shop-visit timing.
- **Related-party governance.** Every stream in §2.5 is between FTAI and an entity it manages and 19%
  owns; pricing policy for aircraft and for MRE exchanges is not disclosed (Unknowns U5, U6).
- **Accounting treatment as a constraint on reported earnings.** Profit on MRE sales to the Partnership
  is deferred and recognized "over time" [S13], so reported Aerospace Products margin on SCI volume is
  lower than on third-party volume in the period of sale.

## 9. Terms introduced

- **Special purpose vehicle (SPV):** a legal entity holding a defined asset pool and its debt, ring-fenced from the sponsor.
- **Limited partner (LP) / general partner (GP):** LP commits capital with limited liability and no management role; GP manages and is liable.
- **Servicer:** the party contracted to manage the assets (leasing, remarketing, collections, technical oversight) for a fee.
- **Closed-end vehicle:** fixed capital, defined investment period, then harvest and wind-up.
- **Equity commitment / hard cap:** a promise to fund when called / the maximum equity the sponsor will accept.
- **Deployment period / harvest phase / fully committed:** buying phase / collecting-and-selling phase / all equity allocated to signed deals.
- **Asset-level debt:** borrowing secured on the aircraft and leases, non-recourse to the sponsor.
- **Warehouse facility:** a revolving acquisition line that funds assets one by one ahead of term refinancing.
- **Accordion:** a pre-agreed option to enlarge a facility.
- **Asset-backed securitization (ABS):** bonds tranched by seniority and repaid from a pool's cash flows.
- **Syndicated / structuring agent / lead arranger:** a loan shared across lenders / the designers and leaders of the deal.
- **Loan-to-cost:** debt ÷ total capital.
- **737NG / A320ceo:** Boeing 737 Next Generation (CFM56-7B) / Airbus A320 current-engine-option family (CFM56-5B or V2500).
- **Mid-life aircraft:** roughly the middle of a 25-30 year economic life, when value concentrates in the engines.
- **On-lease:** already under an operating lease to an airline.
- **Shop visit:** scheduled teardown and restoration of an engine.
- **Module / module exchange / engine exchange:** a separable engine section / swapping a time-expired module or engine for a refurbished one from a pool.
- **MRE (Maintenance, Repair and Exchange):** FTAI's business that supplies exchanges in place of conventional shop visits.
- **Sale-leaseback:** an airline sells aircraft to a lessor and leases them back.
- **Letter of intent (LOI):** a signed but non-binding or conditionally binding agreement to transact.
- **ASC 610-20:** US GAAP standard for gains and losses on derecognition of nonfinancial assets outside ordinary revenue.
- **ASC 606:** US GAAP revenue-recognition standard for contracts with customers.
- **Equity method:** carrying an investment at cost plus share of earnings minus distributions, with the share on one income line.
- **Profit elimination:** removing the investor's share of profit on sales to an equity-method investee until the investee realizes it.
- **Variable interest entity (VIE) / primary beneficiary:** an entity controlled through contracts rather than voting equity / the party that must consolidate it.
- **Waterfall / hurdle (preferred return) / promote (carried interest):** the ordered allocation of distributions / the LP return earned before the sponsor shares / the sponsor's disproportionate profit share.
- **Management fee / servicing fee / incentive fee / sales fee:** fee on capital / fee on rents or assets managed / fee for performance / fee on disposal proceeds.
- **Lease rate factor:** monthly rent ÷ asset value.
- **Anchor investor:** an early, large LP whose commitment seeds a raise.
- **Related-party transaction:** a transaction between a company and an entity it controls, manages or significantly influences.
