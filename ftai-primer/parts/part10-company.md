<div class="part-divider"><div class="pn">Part X</div><h1>FTAI the company</h1><p>This Part describes FTAI Aviation Ltd. as a corporation rather than as a set of businesses: where it came from, who ran it and on what terms, how it is owned and governed, what its consolidated accounts say, how it is financed, what it pays out, what it has promised, and what it discloses about its risks, its auditor and its litigation. ### Translation-table rows answered

Parts IV to IX answer the rows of the Part III translation table directly, by describing a market or a business that a driver stresses. Part X answers them indirectly. A company's capital structure, its cash flow and its guidance are where the drivers show up as money that must be raised, spent or promised. The inventory build described in §81, and the debt that funds it in §82, are the balance-sheet form of the requirement that the low-retirement fleet creates for serviceable CFM56 material (Part III §24, row T-1: low retirements). The guidance history in §84 is management's dated statement of how much of the shop-visit plateau it expects to capture, in dollars of Adjusted EBITDA (the company's profit measure, earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization as the company adjusts it, defined in §80) and in modules (Part III §24, row T-14: shop-visit demand plateau). The margin aspirations inside that guidance, and the CFM materials agreement noted in the timeline, are the company-level response to catalogue escalation by the engine's original equipment manufacturer (OEM) (Part III §24, row T-7: OEM parts escalation). The shift of Aviation Leasing to an asset-light model, and the cut to its 2026 guidance that accompanied it, are the corporate form of the lessor-extension row (Part III §24, row T-6: lease extensions and availability). And the 2027 guidance for FTAI Power is the company's dated statement about the CFM56 cores that the durability problems of the new engines (Pratt & Whitney's GTF geared turbofan and CFM's LEAP, Part III §20) kept flying rather than retiring (Part III §24, rows T-2, T-3: GTF and LEAP durability). None of these rows is answered here with a market mechanism; each is answered with a dated corporate fact, and the Parts named above carry the mechanism.

## 75. History: a dated timeline

FTAI Aviation Ltd. is a Cayman Islands company listed on Nasdaq under the ticker FTAI. It is the successor to a Delaware limited liability company that Fortress Investment Group brought to the New York Stock Exchange in May 2015 under the name Fortress Transportation and Infrastructure Investors LLC. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-K FY2021; FTAI Infrastructure 10-K FY2024]</span> Everything in this Part is documented in filings with the United States Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and in company press releases, so the forms those filings take are defined first.

<div class="defn"><b>SEC filings: Form 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, DEF 14A, DEFM14A and S-4</b><p>A Form 10-K is a US-listed company's audited annual report. A Form 10-Q is its unaudited quarterly report. A Form 8-K is a current report filed within days of a material event, and press releases are usually attached to it as Exhibit 99.1. A DEF 14A is the definitive proxy statement sent before the annual shareholder meeting, carrying director, compensation and ownership disclosure. A DEFM14A is the merger proxy for a transaction put to a shareholder vote. An S-4 is a registration statement for securities issued in a merger or an exchange offer. Foreign private issuers such as AerCap file a Form 20-F and Form 6-K instead (Part XI §87). <span class="cite">[D10 §3, §9; D5 §8]</span></p></div>

The research assembled the dates below from the FY2021, FY2022 and FY2025 annual reports, the quarterly reports of 2024 to 2026, the spin-off and redomiciliation documents, and the company's press releases. Where an event is known only from the orchestrator's chain map or from a filing index rather than from a read document, the table says so.

### 75.1 The Fortress years, 2015 to 2021

The company began as an externally managed investment vehicle, not as an operating business. Fortress Investment Group formed Fortress Transportation and Infrastructure Investors LLC to own transportation and infrastructure assets, and listed it on the New York Stock Exchange on 15 May 2015. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-K FY2021; FTAI Infrastructure 10-K FY2024]</span> A Fortress subsidiary, FIG LLC, supplied the executives, the staff and the investment process under a management agreement, and a Fortress affiliate was entitled to a share of returns above a hurdle. Section 76 describes that structure.

In those years the vehicle owned two kinds of asset. One was aviation: aircraft and engines bought mid-life and leased out, the business that Part VIII §58 describes as Aviation Leasing. The other was infrastructure: a crude-oil and refined-products terminal at Jefferson, Texas; the Repauno site in New Jersey; the Transtar short-line railways; and the Long Ridge power and terminal site in Ohio. Those four names come from the orchestrator's brief; their individual revenues, costs and carrying values were not retrieved by the research, and the FY2021 annual report would supply them. <span class="cite">[D10 §2.2, U-11]</span>

The aviation side began to acquire maintenance capability before the separation. The FY2025 annual report's equity-method note records an "Advanced Engine Repair JV" in which the company invested $15.0M in December 2016 and a further $13.5M in August 2019 for a 25% interest, carried at $22,429K at 31 December 2025. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-K FY2025, via orchestrator notes]</span> Whether that venture is the Chromalloy PMA joint venture through which the annual report says the company "develops and manufactures PMA parts" is not stated in anything retrieved; investor blogs date an "exclusive perpetual partnership" with Chromalloy to 2018, which is neither the 2016 nor the 2019 date. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-K FY2025; Komodo Capital and Kairos Research via D4; reconciliation R-026]</span> Part VI §43 carries that question. The language of the "Module Factory", a maintenance programme at the Lockheed Martin Commercial Engine Solutions facility in Montréal "with a capacity for up to 300 shop visits per year", first appears in the FY2022 annual report and is still carried in the FY2025 one. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-K FY2022; FTAI 10-K FY2025; reconciliation R-037]</span>

### 75.2 The separation, 2022

Three things happened in 2022 that turned the vehicle into the company this primer describes. First, the Russian leases ended. After the invasion of Ukraine the company terminated all its leases with Russian airlines and wrote off $120.0M, net of the maintenance deposits it already held, for the aircraft and engines it did not expect to recover. Eight aircraft and 17 engines were in Russia and four aircraft and one engine were in Ukraine at the end of the year. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-K FY2022]</span> Part VIII §59 carries that episode.

Second, the infrastructure businesses left. A new company, FTAI Infrastructure Inc., was formed on 13 December 2021, and on 1 August 2022 every FTAI shareholder of record on 21 July 2022 received one share of it for each FTAI share held. <span class="cite">[FTAI Infrastructure 10-K FY2024; FTAI 8-K, Aug 2022]</span> Third, the company moved its legal home. On 10 November 2022 the Delaware LLC merged into a Cayman Islands company formed five years earlier, FTAI Aviation Ltd., whose shares the public received automatically. <span class="cite">[FTAI 8-K, Nov 2022; FTAI 10-K FY2022]</span> Section 77 takes the spin-off and the redomicile in turn.

### 75.3 Building the maintenance business and buying out the manager, 2023 to 2024

In 2023 the company bought its first dedicated shop. On 4 January 2023 it acquired the assets of iAero Thrust in Miami together with Unical Aviation as a joint venture, branded the business QuickTurn, and on 1 December 2023 bought Unical's remaining interest for $30.3M in cash. <span class="cite">[GlobeNewswire, 1 Dec 2023; FTAI 10-K, via orchestrator notes]</span> In June 2024 it signed a V2500 maintenance agreement with IAE AG under the EngineWise programme, covering more than 100 shop visits over five years; Part VII §51 describes it. <span class="cite">[RTX newsroom, 6 Jun 2024]</span>

On 28 May 2024 the company ended its external management. It agreed to pay its Former Manager $300.0M, half in cash and half in 1,866,949 new shares, and the Management Agreement terminated that day. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-Q Q3 2024; FTAI 8-K, 28 May 2024]</span> Three and a half months later, on 9 September 2024, it closed the acquisition of Lockheed Martin Commercial Engine Solutions in Montréal for $170.0M, the site that houses the Module Factory. <span class="cite">[GlobeNewswire, 9 Sep 2024; Aviation Week]</span> The research recorded that the orchestrator's chain map and the D10 dossier had originally dated this acquisition to 2023; the release dates the closing to 9 September 2024, and this Part uses that date. <span class="cite">[reconciliation R-039]</span> In October 2024 it redeemed all 4,180,000 of its Series A preferred shares, and on 30 December 2024 it launched the Strategic Capital Initiative and gave 2025 guidance. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-K FY2025; GlobeNewswire, 30 Dec 2024]</span>

### 75.4 The short report, the review and the capital-raising year, 2025

On 15 January 2025 Muddy Waters Research, a short seller, published a report on FTAI's accounting. Three days later the Audit Committee determined to begin a review with independent advisers, and in February 2025 it announced that the allegations were "without merit". <span class="cite">[FTAI 8-K, Jan 2025; FTAI 8-K Ex. 99.1, Feb 2025]</span> Section 85 sets out the wording of each statement and the class action that followed. During the same year the Audit Committee changed the company's auditor, engaging KPMG LLP for FY2025 with effect from 17 June 2025 after Ernst & Young LLP had audited the company from 2016. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-K FY2025, via orchestrator notes]</span>

The rest of 2025 was spent raising capital for the vehicles and adding capacity. The first SCI vehicle secured a $2.5B asset-level debt commitment on 26 February 2025, added One Investment Management as a partner on 25 March, and closed its equity at an upsized $2.0B hard cap on 27 October. <span class="cite">[GlobeNewswire, 26 Feb 2025, 25 Mar 2025 and 27 Oct 2025]</span> Part VIII §62 carries the capital. The QuickTurn Europe joint venture at Rome Fiumicino closed on 5 June 2025 at $10.5M for 50%, and on 30 December 2025 the company launched FTAI Power. <span class="cite">[GlobeNewswire, 5 Jun 2025 and 30 Dec 2025]</span>

### 75.5 Guidance, redemptions and buybacks, 2026

The first nine months of 2026 brought a materials agreement with CFM International (22 January), the FY2025 results and 2026 guidance of $1.625B (25 February), the 10-K and auditor's report (27 February), and a new Chief Financial Officer (6 March). <span class="cite">[GlobeNewswire, 22 Jan 2026, 25 Feb 2026 and 6 Mar 2026; FTAI 10-K FY2025]</span> In May the company announced the full redemption of its Series C preferred shares and priced what its release called an inaugural asset-backed securitization; the research located both titles and read neither body. <span class="cite">[GlobeNewswire, 15 May 2026 and 22 May 2026, titles]</span> In July J&F Power Systems received a $1.465B initial order, and the Q2 2026 results cut 2026 Aviation Leasing guidance to $475M while setting 2027 guidance at $2.3B. <span class="cite">[FTAI release, 22 Jul 2026; GlobeNewswire, 29 Jul 2026]</span> August brought the $2.0B warehouse for the second vehicle, September a $500M repurchase authorisation and the purchase of 27 Boeing 737-700s from WestJet. <span class="cite">[GlobeNewswire, 17 Aug 2026, 15 Sep 2026 and 28 Sep 2026]</span> Sections 82 to 84 take the financing, the payouts and the guidance in detail.

## 76. External management and the 2024 internalization

For its first nine years as a listed company FTAI had no employees of its own. That arrangement, and the price of ending it, shape how the company's expense lines read before and after mid-2024.

<div class="defn"><b>External management: manager, management agreement, management fee, incentive allocation and Master GP</b><p>External management is a structure in which a listed company has no staff of its own. A separate firm, the manager, supplies the executives, the employees and the investment process under a management agreement, and is paid a management fee, typically a percentage of the company's equity or assets. At FTAI the manager was FIG LLC, a subsidiary of Fortress Investment Group. A performance payment, the incentive allocation, went to a Fortress affiliate that the filings call the Master GP (GP, general partner) under a separate Services and Profit Sharing Agreement; it was a share of distributable returns above a hurdle. The entity name "Fortress Worldwide Transportation and Infrastructure Master GP LLC" comes from the orchestrator's chain map and was not re-verified. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-Q Q3 2024; FTAI 8-K, 28 May 2024; D10 §2.1]</span></p></div>

<div class="defn"><b>Fortress, FIG LLC and FTAI Infrastructure (FIP)</b><p>Fortress Investment Group is the alternative asset manager that sponsored FTAI's 2015 listing. FIG LLC, its subsidiary, was FTAI's external manager until 28 May 2024 and is called the "Former Manager" in the filings since. FTAI Infrastructure Inc., Nasdaq ticker FIP, is the company that received FTAI's infrastructure businesses on 1 August 2022; it appears in this primer only as history. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-Q Q3 2024; FTAI Infrastructure 10-K FY2024]</span></p></div>

### 76.1 The two agreements

Two contracts governed the relationship. The Management Agreement set the manager's fee and its duties. The Services and Profit Sharing Agreement set the incentive allocation payable to the Master GP. The Q3 2024 quarterly report names both as the agreements under which "accrued but not yet paid compensation and reimbursable expenses" were owed at termination. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-Q Q3 2024]</span>

The economics of the agreements were sought and not retrieved. The management fee rate and its base, the incentive allocation formula, and the amounts paid in 2021, 2022, 2023 and the first five months of 2024 are all unknown in the research; the related-party notes of the FY2021 to FY2024 annual reports would supply them. <span class="cite">[D10 U-5]</span> The dossier offered one lead, a fee of 1.50% a year of total equity, and labelled it a recollection, unverified. It is repeated here only with that label and is used in no arithmetic. The practical consequence for a reader of the accounts is that the research cannot say how much of FTAI's pre-2024 operating expense was the fee and the allocation, and therefore cannot restate 2023 on a post-internalization basis.

One further open point sits in a later filing. The research flagged text about "management fees and incentive allocations" in the Q1 2025 quarterly report and could not determine whether it refers to the terminated Fortress arrangements or to the Strategic Capital vehicles' fee structure; Part VIII §63 carries the vehicle side. <span class="cite">[D7 U17; reconciliation C.7 item 6]</span>

### 76.2 The internalization of 28 May 2024

<div class="defn"><b>Internalization</b><p>Internalization is the termination of an external management agreement, the hiring of the manager's people directly by the company, and a payment to the manager for the fee stream it gives up. FTAI's closed on 28 May 2024 for a stated $300.0M: $150.0M in cash paid on 17 June 2024 and 1,866,949 ordinary shares valued at $150.0M at closing, about $80.35 a share, plus accrued but unpaid compensation, reimbursable expenses and "certain other expenses". After that date management fees and incentive distributions ceased and were replaced by salaries, bonuses and equity awards, so operating expenses before and after mid-2024 are not like for like. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-Q Q3 2024; FTAI 10-Q Q2 2024; FTAI 8-K, 28 May 2024]</span></p></div>

The mechanics, as the filings describe them, are these. On 28 May 2024 FTAI entered into definitive agreements with the Former Manager and the Master GP. The Management Agreement terminated with effect from that date, after which "the Company was no longer required to pay management fees or incentive distributions". <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-Q Q3 2024]</span> The consideration, in the quarterly report's words: "the Company agreed to pay $300.0 million to its Former Manager (for itself and on behalf of the Master GP, as applicable), issued 1,866,949 ordinary shares valued at $150.0 million at closing, with the remaining balance paid in cash on June 17, 2024". <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-Q Q3 2024; FTAI 10-Q Q2 2024]</span> The 8-K adds that the company also paid all accrued but unpaid compensation and reimbursable expenses under both agreements, plus certain other expenses. <span class="cite">[FTAI 8-K, 28 May 2024]</span>

### 76.3 What the research did not retrieve about the transaction

The task for this Part asked for the lock-up on the shares issued, the transition-services arrangements, the special committee that negotiated the deal and its advisers. None of these appears in the material the research retrieved. The 8-K of 28 May 2024 and its exhibits, and the June 2024 8-Ks that the research located but did not read, are where a lock-up or transition-services agreement would be filed and where the board's process would be described. <span class="cite">[FTAI 8-K, 28 May 2024; FTAI 8-Ks, June 2024, located not read; D10 U-6]</span> This Part therefore records those four items as unknown rather than describing them.

<div class="box"><div class="h">Why the internalization changes how the expense lines read</div><p>Before 28 May 2024 the cost of running the company reached the income statement as a management fee and, when earned, an incentive allocation to a Fortress affiliate. ## 77. The 2022 spin-off and the redomicile

Two transactions in the second half of 2022 define the entity whose accounts the rest of this Part describes. The first removed the infrastructure businesses. The second changed the parent company's country and legal form.

### 77.1 The spin-off of FTAI Infrastructure

<div class="defn"><b>Spin-off and record date</b><p>A spin-off is a pro-rata distribution of a subsidiary's shares to the parent's shareholders, after which the subsidiary is a separate listed company. The record date is the day on which a shareholder must hold the parent's shares to receive the distribution. FTAI distributed one share of FTAI Infrastructure Inc. for each FTAI share held at the close of business on 21 July 2022; the distribution took place on 1 August 2022 and FTAI Infrastructure began trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market as "FIP". <span class="cite">[FTAI Infrastructure 10-K FY2024; FTAI 8-K, Aug 2022]</span></p></div>

FTAI Infrastructure was formed as a limited liability company on 13 December 2021 to hold the infrastructure businesses. <span class="cite">[FTAI Infrastructure 10-K FY2024]</span> What went to it, per the orchestrator's brief, were the Jefferson Terminal, Repauno, Transtar and Long Ridge businesses; the research did not retrieve their figures, and the FY2021 annual report of the parent would carry them. <span class="cite">[D10 §2.2, U-11]</span> What stayed was aviation: the leasing fleet and the early maintenance assets described in §75.1.

<div class="defn"><b>Discontinued operations</b><p>Discontinued operations is the accounting presentation under which a business that a company has disposed of, or is about to dispose of, is shown as a single net line below the results of the continuing business, with prior periods restated the same way. FTAI's FY2021 and FY2022 statements present the infrastructure businesses this way, so the net losses of those years ($129.0M in 2021 and $220.4M in 2022) are not comparable with the FY2023 and later results of the continuing aviation business without restatement. The D10 dossier used the term without defining it; this definition is the primer's. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-K FY2022, via orchestrator notes; D10 §2.2; registry section 2]</span></p></div>

### 77.2 The redomiciliation to the Cayman Islands

<div class="defn"><b>Cayman Islands exempted company and redomiciliation</b><p>A Cayman Islands exempted company is a company incorporated in the Cayman Islands that conducts its business mainly outside the islands; it is a common holding-company form for aircraft lessors. Redomiciliation is the move of a company's legal home from one jurisdiction to another. FTAI did it by merger: FTAI Aviation Ltd., formed in the Cayman Islands on 8 December 2017, became the surviving parent on completion of an Agreement and Plan of Merger dated 10 November 2022 with Fortress Transportation and Infrastructure Investors LLC, a Delaware limited liability company, whose public shares "were exchanged automatically for shares of FTAI Aviation Ltd." <span class="cite">[FTAI 8-K, Nov 2022; FTAI 10-K FY2022]</span></p></div>

The merger was put to shareholders in a definitive merger proxy, a DEFM14A, in 2022. <span class="cite">[FTAI DEFM14A 2022]</span> In the same year an entity named FTAI Finance Holdco Ltd., SEC registrant number (CIK, the Central Index Key the SEC assigns to each filer) 1942420, filed a Form S-4, which is consistent with exchanging the old issuer's notes for notes of the new issuer. <span class="cite">[FTAI Finance Holdco Ltd. Form S-4, 2022]</span> The same entity filed a Schedule 13G/A, the SEC form on which a passive holder reports beneficial ownership of more than 5% of a class of shares, in 2026; its current role, whether as a note co-issuer or otherwise, was not confirmed. <span class="cite">[D10 §3.1, U-13]</span>

The task for this Part asked for the tax and governance consequences of the redomicile as the filings state them. The company's stated reasons are in the DEFM14A, and the research did not retrieve them; nor did it retrieve the reasons for the later move of the ordinary shares from the New York Stock Exchange to Nasdaq, where they now trade. <span class="cite">[D10 U-12]</span> What can be said from the material retrieved is structural. The parent is a Cayman company and the ordinary shares are its shares; the board is a classified board of seven; the proxy applies Nasdaq's independence rules (§78). The DEFM14A of 2022 and the listing-transfer 8-K are the documents that would supply the rest, and this Part records the gap rather than filling it.

<div class="box"><div class="h">Why 2021 and 2022 are not a baseline</div><p>The figures of 2021 and 2022 describe a different company. They include the infrastructure businesses in discontinued operations, they include Russian lease revenue of about $39.8M in 2021 and its loss in 2022, they include the $120.0M Russia write-off, and they were produced under an external manager whose fee sat in the expense lines. The FY2023 figures are the first full year of the continuing aviation company, and the FY2025 annual report's three-year segment table (§79) begins there. Any growth rate computed from 2022 to 2023 therefore compares two differently constituted companies and is not reported in this Part. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-K FY2022; FTAI 10-K FY2025; D10 §2.2]</span></p></div>

## 80. Adjusted EBITDA: the definition and the reconciliation

FTAI reports its results, sets its guidance and describes its segments in a measure it defines itself. Every Part of this primer that quotes an FTAI profit figure is quoting this measure, so its construction matters.

<div class="defn"><b>Adjusted EBITDA (consolidated; the company's definition)</b><p>EBITDA is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. Adjusted EBITDA is FTAI's company-defined variant. It removes items management treats as non-recurring or non-cash and adds back the company's pro-rata share of Adjusted EBITDA from its unconsolidated, equity-method investments. The research expected the reconciling items to include equity-based compensation, acquisition and transaction costs, internalization costs, asset impairments, changes in the fair value of non-hedge derivatives and dividends on preferred shares, with the bridge starting from net income attributable to shareholders; the exact FY2025 definition and the line-by-line reconciliation were in the annual report and the earnings release and were not returned by the searches. FTAI reports the measure at consolidated level ($597.3M in 2023, $862.1M in 2024, $1,190.9M in 2025; $291.4M in Q2 2026) and by segment, and sets guidance in it. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-K FY2025; FTAI release, 29 Jul 2026; D10 §2.5, U-1]</span></p></div>

### 80.1 What is known about the construction

Four things about the measure are established in the filings retrieved. First, it is reported both consolidated and by segment, and the segment figures are the ones guidance is expressed in (§84). <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-K FY2025; FTAI release, 29 Jul 2026]</span> Second, it includes the company's share of the Adjusted EBITDA of its equity-method investees, so the 2025 Partnership's earnings reach FTAI's Adjusted EBITDA in proportion to its 19% (or 20%) stake even though the Partnership's revenue does not reach FTAI's revenue. <span class="cite">[D10 §2.5; D7 §2.6; reconciliation R-048]</span> Third, in the Aviation Leasing segment the measure adds back depreciation and includes gains on the sale of leasing equipment, so a year in which the segment sells many aircraft carries the gains inside its Adjusted EBITDA; the FY2025 gain on sales to the 2025 Partnership was $46.4M, against a nine-month sum of quarterly gains of $50.1M, a tension Part VIII §63 carries. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-K FY2025; D6 §2.6; D7 §4.2; reconciliation R-054]</span> Fourth, the measure is stated before preferred dividends, so it is before the roughly $6.2M a year of Series D dividends and before the Series B and Series C dividends while those were outstanding (§82). <span class="cite">[D10 §2.5]</span>

A fifth point sits beside those four and is recorded as the filing states it. The annual report describes the aircraft sales to the 2025 Partnership as accounted for under ASC 610-20 (the Accounting Standards Codification topic for gains on sales of non-financial assets) and as "non-recurring in nature and not considered part of the Company's ordinary activities", while the launch release describes a programme of "$3.0+ billion of capital annually" and the Q2 2026 quarterly report says the company's primary investment activity will run through the Strategic Capital Initiative going forward. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-K FY2025; GlobeNewswire, 30 Dec 2024; FTAI 10-Q Q2 2026; reconciliation R-057]</span> The strategy language and the accounting language describe the same sales differently, and Part VIII §64 sets them side by side.

### 80.2 Segment and total: two bases

The registry records a conflict between the segment measure and the consolidated one, and between FTAI's measure and the peers'. <span class="cite">[registry C-24]</span> The segment measure, as the D6 dossier describes it for Aviation Leasing, "adds back depreciation and includes gains and the equity-method pick-up". The consolidated measure, as the D10 dossier describes it, is the company-defined total after the removal of items treated as non-recurring or non-cash and the add-back of the pro-rata share from unconsolidated investments and of preferred dividends. The two segments' Adjusted EBITDA do not sum to the consolidated figure; the difference is the Corporate and Other column, which carries the costs of the parent and whatever adjustments are not allocated to a segment.

### 80.3 Comparability with other companies

Part XI §88 compares FTAI's segment Adjusted EBITDA margin with the measures the peers report, and it begins by saying that no two of those measures are on the same basis. StandardAero reports "Segment Adjusted EBITDA", its own defined measure; HEICO reports segment operating income after depreciation; MTU and Lufthansa Technik report adjusted EBIT (earnings before interest and taxes); GE Aerospace reports segment profit; AerSale reports gross profit. <span class="cite">[D11 §2.3, §2.5]</span> An EBITDA margin exceeds an EBIT margin by the share of revenue that depreciation and amortization represents, which at FTAI was $225.8M on $2,507.4M of revenue in 2025, or 9.0 points. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-K FY2025; arithmetic]</span> That is the size of the gap a reader opens by setting FTAI's 34.7% Aerospace Products margin beside an EBIT-based peer margin without adjustment, and it is why this Part states the basis every time it quotes the measure.

## 81. Cash flow and the inventory build

FTAI reported net income in 2024 and 2025 and consumed cash in its operations in both years. The reason is a feature of the cash flow statement that is worth setting out from first principles.

<div class="defn"><b>Operating versus investing cash flow: inventory against leasing equipment</b><p>Under US GAAP (generally accepted accounting principles) the cash a company spends buying assets it intends to sell is an operating outflow, because those assets are inventory and sit in working capital. The cash it spends buying assets it intends to lease is an investing outflow, because those assets are leasing equipment, capitalised and depreciated over time. A company that buys engines, modules and parts to refurbish and sell therefore shows its purchases as operating outflows, and its operating cash flow will be negative while its inventory is growing faster than its sales even if it reports a profit. FTAI's net cash from operating activities was +$129.0M in 2023, −$188.0M in 2024 and −$310.7M in 2025. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-K FY2025; D10 §2.6]</span></p></div>

## 82. Debt, preferred shares and leverage

FTAI finances itself with five series of senior unsecured notes, an undrawn bank revolver, and perpetual preferred shares, of which two series have been redeemed. This section sets out each instrument, the one tension in the debt figures, and three illustrations that are labelled as such because the company's own leverage definitions and its credit ratings were not retrieved.

### 82.1 The senior notes

<div class="defn"><b>Senior unsecured notes; bullet; callable</b><p>Senior unsecured notes are bonds that rank ahead of equity and subordinated debt in a liquidation but are not backed by specific collateral. ### 82.2 The revolver

<div class="defn"><b>Revolving credit facility and SOFR</b><p>A revolving credit facility, or revolver, is a bank line the company may draw and repay repeatedly up to a committed amount. It pays a floating interest rate on drawn balances and a commitment fee on the undrawn portion. FTAI's prices drawings at the banks' base rate plus 1.75% or at Adjusted Term SOFR plus 2.75%, where SOFR is the Secured Overnight Financing Rate, the US dollar benchmark that replaced LIBOR, the London Interbank Offered Rate. The facility was undrawn at 31 December 2025 and matures on 22 May 2027; its commitment size was not retrieved. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-K FY2025; D10 §2.7, U-3]</span></p></div>

Two maturities fall inside the guidance horizon of §84. The revolver matures on 22 May 2027, and the 5.50% notes, about $1.0B, on 1 May 2028. <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-K FY2025]</span> The terms on which either is renewed or refinanced will depend in part on the company's credit ratings, which the research did not retrieve, and on the company's leverage covenants, which it also did not retrieve. <span class="cite">[D10 U-7]</span> This Part records both as unknown and does not characterise them.

### 82.4 The preferred shares

<div class="defn"><b>Preferred shares: cumulative, perpetual, redeemable; liquidation preference</b><p>Preferred shares are equity that ranks ahead of the ordinary shares for dividends and in a liquidation and normally carries no vote. <div class="defn"><b>Fixed-to-floating and fixed-rate reset preferred</b><p>Two coupon designs appear. ### 85.1 Risk factors

<div class="defn"><b>Risk factors (Item 1A); auditor; securities class action; lead plaintiff</b><p>Item 1A of a Form 10-K is the company's own list of the risks it considers material, each with a heading and an explanatory paragraph. ### 85.3 The Audit Committee review of 2025

The sequence is dated by the filings. Muddy Waters Research published its report on 15 January 2025. On 18 January 2025 the Audit Committee "determined to commence a review (which included the engagement of independent advisors) in response to the assertions", in the January 8-K's words as the D10 dossier read it; the D5 dossier's reading of the same 8-K is that the committee "determined to begin a review, which included engaging independent advisors". <span class="cite">[FTAI 8-K, Jan 2025; D10 §2.11; D5 §2.9]</span> The conclusion was announced in February 2025; trade coverage of 20 February 2025 reported the shares rising on it, which dates the announcement to on or about 19 or 20 February. <span class="cite">[FTAI 8-K Ex. 99.1, Feb 2025; StockStory via Wedbush, 20 Feb 2025]</span>

The wording of the conclusion differs by document, and the reconciliation pass asked that the versions be kept apart. The February 8-K exhibit, as D5 read it: "After a thorough and comprehensive review with the support of independent legal and forensic accounting advisors, the Audit Committee determined that the allegations made against the Company are without merit. The Company expects to file its Form 10-K timely." <span class="cite">[FTAI 8-K Ex. 99.1, Feb 2025, via D5]</span> The same exhibit, as D10 read it: the committee "completed its review, conducted by independent legal and forensic accounting advisors ... and determined that the allegations made against the Company are without merit". <span class="cite">[FTAI 8-K Ex. 99.1, Feb 2025, via D10]</span> The Q3 2025 quarterly report: "the internal investigation concluded that the allegations of misconduct were all without merit". <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-Q Q3 2025]</span> The FY2025 annual report, as the orchestrator notes transcribe it: an Audit Committee internal investigation, with outside counsel and forensic accountants, into the January 2025 short-seller reports concluded the allegations of misconduct were "all without merit". <span class="cite">[FTAI 10-K FY2025, via orchestrator notes]</span> The 8-K speaks of a review and of allegations; the later filings speak of an internal investigation and of allegations of misconduct; and the annual report speaks of reports in the plural. This Part does not fuse them. <span class="cite">[reconciliation R-063; E.1 rule 22]</span>

The allegations themselves are carried as two secondary summaries, because the report was not read. As the amended complaint states them, per law-firm case pages: one-time engine sales reported as MRO (maintenance, repair and overhaul) revenue; whole engine sales presented as module sales; and depreciation of engines not on lease lowering reported cost of goods sold. <span class="cite">[ktmc.com and rgrdlaw.com case pages, via orchestrator notes]</span> As the press summarised the report in January 2025: one-time engine sales recorded as MRO revenue in Aerospace Products; the majority of the segment's Adjusted EBITDA estimated to come from gains on sales; and "misrepresentation of sales, inflation of ... margins ..., channel stuffing, and a few other accounting red flags". <span class="cite">[Benzinga, 15 Jan 2025; Crossroads Capital letter via Hedge Fund Alpha; D5 §2.9; reconciliation R-064]</span> Part VII §54 sets the allegations beside the segment's disclosures. No restatement was found or referenced in anything retrieved, and the FY2025 annual report presents MRE Contract revenue as a line separate from Aerospace products revenue, a change the research could not link to the review. <span class="cite">[D5 §2.9]</span>

### 85.5 Employees and properties

<div class="defn"><b>Employees (headcount as the 10-K defines it)</b><p>FTAI reports 985 "full-time employees and independent contractors" at 31 December 2025, a single figure that mixes two categories, and separately 494 full-time employees in Canada, about 71% of whom are covered by collective bargaining agreements.
