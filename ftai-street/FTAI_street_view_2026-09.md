## Summary

Both rated notes are Buys with targets roughly 70–80% above the price: Morgan Stanley at $360 (Overweight, its Top Pick in Aerospace) and Jefferies at $325 (Buy). Per the consensus data they cite, the rest of the covering street is also unanimously positive. The bull case is a 2027 earnings step-up: management's ~$2.3B segment EBITDA guide is roughly 60–80% above 2026. It rests on three legs: Aerospace Products modules (the core business), the new FTAI Power turbine business, and the asset-light SCI leasing vehicle.

The live disagreements are about how much Power to believe, whether ~30% Aerospace margins are a floor or a new normal, and whether 2026 leasing guidance is reachable. They are also about the near-term setup. Jefferies sits below consensus for Q3 and calls 2H26 back-end loaded. Morgan Stanley cut its 2026 EPS by ~19% while raising 2027 by ~33%.

Valuation is the street's main argument. Jefferies has FTAI at ~10x next-year EBITDA, a 24% discount to its own 3-year average and a ~44% discount to aftermarket peers. Yet the options market priced only a ~1% chance of reaching MS's $360 target and ~45% of falling below its $155 bear case within 12 months (MS, as of Aug 20).

## Narrative

### 1. Consensus, targets and ratings (3a)

The covering street is unanimously positive. Both notes cite consensus data with no Hold or Sell ratings: MS (Refinitiv) shows 100% Overweight, and Jefferies' comp table shows 100% Buy [ST-01]. The consensus price target averages ~$367, in a $290–600 range [ST-02]. The two firms here sit below that average. MS raised its target from $319 to $360 after Q2 [ST-03]; Jefferies holds $325 [ST-04].

(A "price target" is the analyst's 12–18-month value estimate. "Consensus" is the average of all covering analysts' published estimates.)

For 2027, the year everyone's thesis rests on, consensus EBITDA is ~$2.18B per Jefferies (VisibleAlpha) or ~$2.30B per MS (Refinitiv). Refinitiv's range runs from $2.10B to $2.92B [ST-05]. The two firms bracket it:
- MS models ~$2.32B.
- Jefferies models $2.05B, below consensus.

On EPS the split is the same [ST-06]:
- MS 2027 EPS: $13.02.
- Jefferies 2027 EPS: $10.25.
- Consensus 2027 EPS: $12.92–13.36.

For 2026, consensus EBITDA is ~$1.32B; MS is ~$1.3B and Jefferies $1.27B [ST-05].

### 2. Estimate direction (3a)

Estimates are moving in two directions: 2026 down, 2027 and later up.
- **MS (after Q2):**
  - 2026 EPS cut from $6.77 to $5.48 and 2026 EBITDA from ~$1.5B to ~$1.3B, reflecting the lower leasing outlook.
  - 2027 EPS raised from $9.78 to $13.02 and 2028 from $12.27 to $16.71, as it brought Power into its model.
  - Q3 EPS estimate cut from $1.54 to $1.31 and Q4 from $2.61 to $1.75 [ST-07].
- **Jefferies (Sep 23):** cut Q3 adjusted EBITDA ~5% to $302M, against consensus of $332M [ST-08].

### 3. The bull case as the street makes it (3b)

**Morgan Stanley** frames FTAI as a multi-engine growth story with "multiple upside levers across Power, Aerospace Products, and SCI." It sees the risk/reward skewed ~8.6:1 [ST-09]. Its points:
- **Power:** the market misread the $450M 2027 Power guide. Management clarified that it assumes "materially fewer than 100 units" and is the low end of a $450–750M range, with unit economics intact (~$1M per megawatt, $7–8M EBITDA per unit). MS models $693M, near the top of that range. It argues the $1.465B anchor order and the advance and milestone payments reduce the risk in the 2027 ramp [ST-10].
- **Aerospace Products:** MS calls the margin pressure "a constructive tradeoff for larger, stickier Tier 1 airline customers." It points to share gains (~14%, up from ~12%) and module output (296 in Q2; targets of ~1,200 for 2026 and ~1,700 for 2027) [ST-11].
- **SCI:** MS sees the leasing guide cut as "near-term noise around a strategically positive transition." Balance-sheet leasing is shrinking faster than SCI ramps, and SCI should drive most leasing earnings by 4Q26. (SCI, the Strategic Capital Initiative, is a third-party-funded vehicle that buys leased aircraft; FTAI earns fees and a co-investment share.) [ST-12].
- **Bull case:** $565, on 2027 EBITDA of $3.0B at ~20x. This assumes 35% of modules carry FAA-approved replacement (PMA) parts and PMA supplies ~16% of EBITDA. (PMA, Parts Manufacturer Approval, lets non-OEM parts replace pricier OEM parts.) [ST-13].

**Jefferies** describes FTAI as "a vertically integrated aftermarket power provider" for the CFM56 and V2500 engines [ST-14]. Its points:
- **Share:** the company has ~10% (2025) of a ~$25B aftermarket for those engines, heading toward 25%. That is backed by customer adoption and the "captive market" SCI creates [ST-14].
- **Valuation:** the stock is too cheap. Its sum-of-the-parts (valuing each segment separately) prices Aerospace Products at 17x, Power at 18x, SCI at ~15x like an asset manager, and core leasing at 9x. That gets to $325 [ST-15].
- **Discounts:** FTAI trades at a 38% discount to aftermarket peers on 2027 P/E and 44% on EV/EBITDA [ST-16]. It is also ~24% below its own 3-year average multiples [ST-17].
- **Buyback:** the $500M program is "firepower to spend FCF" with shares at ~10x [ST-18].
- **Upside case:** $550, on 25% share, 100 turbines at $1M/MW, and Aerospace margins of 40%+ from PMA, CFM blade savings, repairs, pricing and the Palantir partnership [ST-19].

**Deutsche Bank** (no rating) highlights a new expansion route [ST-20]:
- Vietjet signed an agreement with FTAI and is jointly assessing a Vietnam QuickTurn engine-maintenance (MRE) platform at Long Thanh airport.
- DB calls GTF engines "the next natural fit" after the LEAP entry FTAI plans for 2028–29. It sees partnerships as "step one" into next-generation engines.

### 4. The bear case as the street makes it (3b)

None of the three notes is bearish; the bear arguments appear only as scenarios and risk lists inside the Buy notes.

**MS bear case: $155.**
- 2027 EBITDA of $1.6B at ~11x.
- Slower PMA adoption (~5% of modules).
- **No value for FTAI Power** [ST-13].
- Listed downside risks: PMA parts not approved, engine-leasing utilization staying below historical levels, failure to raise further partnerships, and Power earning a low return [ST-21].

**Jefferies downside: $150.**
- 2027 EBITDA of $1.4B at 15.5x.
- OEMs accelerating new-engine production. That would speed CFM56 retirements and cut lease rates and residual values.
- Slowing adoption of modular maintenance and PMA.
- Failure to win Power customers at scale.
- Competition as the fleet moves to next-generation engines [ST-19].

**Near-term caution from Jefferies, in the Buy note itself:**
- It "struggle[s]" to reach the $475M 2026 leasing guide and models $446M.
- First-half leasing EBITDA of $241M included $60M of gains and insurance. Assuming core leasing stays at ~$50M a quarter, SCI would need to contribute $134M in 2H vs $60M in 1H [ST-22].
- Q3 Aerospace Products is "weighted… more to Q4": Q4 Aerospace EBITDA of ~$310M is implied to hit the $1.05B guide [ST-08].

**DB's fuel-stress datapoint:** Volotea, an A319/A320ceo operator, is restructuring "primarily due to the significant rise in fuel costs." FTAI has 3 A319s with it, vs AerCap's 13 [ST-23].

### 5. Where the street disagrees with itself (3b, reported, not resolved)

- **How much Power to count.** For 2027:
  - MS models Power EBITDA of $693M, near the top of management's $450–750M range.
  - Jefferies uses $450M, which it describes as "the high-confidence #," implying ~60 of 100 units at ~$7.5M each.
  - MS's own bear case assigns Power zero.

  This one line accounts for much of the $2.05B vs $2.32B gap in 2027 EBITDA [ST-10, ST-24].
- **Aerospace margins.**
  - Both relay management's ~30% guide. Jefferies notes management says its current customer mix (2–3 customers at 50%+ of the mix) will prove ~30% "durable" [ST-24].
  - MS's base case has PMA at only ~5% of 2027 EBITDA.
  - Jefferies' upside case needs 40%+ margins.

  So the margin path from here is an open scenario variable, not a settled view [ST-13, ST-19].
- **Leasing.**
  - 2026: Jefferies is below guide at $446M; consensus is $467M against the $475M guide.
  - 2027: MS has cut its estimate to ~$433M, below management's ~$450M. Jefferies is above it at $481M [ST-22, ST-25].
- **Valuation framing.**
  - MS uses a ~17x blended EV/EBITDA, raised from ~15x, and dropped its probability-weighted DCF once Power entered the model.
  - Jefferies averages 18x EV/EBITDA and 31x P/E. It justifies a 39% premium to the S&P on EV/EBITDA, against a 3-year average 7% discount [ST-03, ST-15].

### 6. Sentiment and positioning (3a where measured)

- **Ratings:** unanimous Buy/Overweight per the consensus data cited [ST-01].
- **Morgan Stanley's quant models** are split by horizon. The 3-month model scores FTAI most favored (1/5); the 24-month model scores it least favored (5/5) [ST-26].
- **Options market:** implied probabilities at Aug 20 put ~1.2% odds on reaching $360 and ~45% on falling below $155 within 12 months. (These are risk-neutral probabilities from option prices, not forecasts.) [ST-27].
- **Ownership:**
  - ~69% of institutional holders are active managers [ST-26].
  - Float is 97.3% and average daily value traded is ~$246M (JEF) [ST-04].
  - MS's hedge-fund long/short ratio (1.4x) and net exposure (8%) are **sector-level**, not FTAI-specific [ST-26].
- **Price backdrop:**
  - Both notes cite a 52-week high of $323.51.
  - The 52-week low is $138.06 (MS) or $149.50 (JEF), because each window runs from a different date.
  - The stock went from $197.81 (Aug 20) to $189.74 (Sep 23) between the two notes [ST-28].
- **No short-interest data** in any of the three notes (see unknowns).

### 7. What the street is watching (3b)

- **The Q3 print**, with Jefferies vs consensus:
  - Adjusted EBITDA $302M vs $332M.
  - Aerospace Products $268M vs $276M; margin 29.9% vs 31.8%.
  - Leasing $90M vs $108M.
  - EPS $1.30 vs $1.56.
  - Revenue $984M vs $966M.
  - Production of 305 modules [ST-08].
- **Power execution:**
  - Mod-1 testing, now on an operating gen-set in Miami.
  - The 4Q26 commercial launch.
  - Further customer orders beyond the $1.465B anchor.
  - How Power is reported: MS says it will be its own segment. Jefferies says FTAI books 100% of the EBITDA on the turbine sale to the J&F joint venture, then 25–50% of the JV's margin on the sale to the customer [ST-10, ST-24].
- **SCI:** whether the SCI II raise reaches $6B (FTAI commits 15%), and how fast its earnings ramp. SCI I is now in "harvest mode" after a $612M ABS-funded special distribution [ST-12].
- **Capacity and footprint:** physical capacity is ~3,000 modules a year. New sites include Jakarta, Cairo, Lisbon and a Rome test cell able to handle LEAP. There is also the AEI cargo-conversion channel. LEAP maintenance is relevant from 2028–29 and is seen as 2–3x the size of the CFM56 market [ST-11].
- **Buyback execution** and the ~$878M 2026 free-cash-flow guide. Jefferies notes $255M (29%) came in the first half, before any Power milestone cash [ST-18].
- **Lessee stress from fuel prices** (Volotea) and new-engine partnerships (Vietjet) [ST-20, ST-23].
